# 7.16.4 RX Performance


![RX Performance Graphs](./images/page_30_diagram.png)


**Detailed Description of Figures**

This image contains six graphs illustrating the RX (Receiver) performance of the CC1312R7 device under various conditions.

*   **Figure 7-10. Sensitivity vs. Frequency (50 kbps, 868 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** Line graph.
    *   **Y-axis:** Sensitivity [dBm], from -115 to -105.
    *   **X-axis:** Frequency [MHz], from 863 to 870.
    *   **Description:** This graph shows that the receiver sensitivity is very stable across the 868 MHz frequency band, consistently holding at approximately -110.5 dBm for a 50 kbps data rate.

*   **Figure 7-11. Sensitivity vs. Frequency (50 kbps, 915 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** Line graph.
    *   **Y-axis:** Sensitivity [dBm], from -115 to -105.
    *   **X-axis:** Frequency [MHz], from 900 to 930.
    *   **Description:** Similar to the 868 MHz band, this graph shows stable receiver sensitivity across the 915 MHz band, hovering just below -110 dBm for a 50 kbps data rate.

*   **Figure 7-12. Sensitivity vs. Temperature (50 kbps, 868.3 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** Line graph.
    *   **Y-axis:** Sensitivity [dBm], from -115 to -105.
    *   **X-axis:** Temperature [°C], from -40 to 100.
    *   **Description:** This graph plots sensitivity against ambient temperature. The sensitivity is approximately -111.5 dBm at -40°C and gradually becomes less sensitive (a higher dBm value) as temperature increases, reaching about -109 dBm at 100°C. The performance remains relatively stable across the full temperature range.

*   **Figure 7-13. Sensitivity vs. Supply Voltage (VDDS) (50 kbps, 868.3 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** Line graph.
    *   **Y-axis:** Sensitivity [dBm], from -115 to -105.
    *   **X-axis:** Voltage [V], from 1.8 to 3.8.
    *   **Description:** This graph shows that receiver sensitivity is extremely stable across the entire supply voltage range (VDDS), maintaining a constant level of approximately -110.5 dBm.

*   **Figure 7-14. Selectivity vs. Frequency Offset (50 kbps, 868.3 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** Line graph.
    *   **Y-axis:** Selectivity [dB], from -20 to 80.
    *   **X-axis:** Frequency [MHz], from -10 to 10.
    *   **Description:** This plot illustrates the receiver's channel selectivity. It shows a sharp notch at 0 MHz offset, indicating the receiver is tuned to the desired channel. The selectivity is high (over 60 dB) for interfering signals at frequency offsets greater than approximately +/- 2 MHz, demonstrating strong rejection of adjacent and alternate channel interference.

*   **Figure 7-15. PER vs. Level vs. Frequency (SimpleLink™ Long Range 5 kbps, 868 MHz)**
    *   **Type:** 2D color map (heat map).
    *   **Y-axis:** Level [dBm], from -120 to 0.
    *   **X-axis:** Offset frequency [ppm], from -30 to 30.
    *   **Color Scale:** Packet Error Rate (PER), from 0 (dark) to 100 (light/white).
    *   **Description:** This graph visualizes the Packet Error Rate (PER) as a function of both signal level and frequency offset for a 5 kbps Long Range mode. The dark, U-shaped area represents a low PER (good reception). This operational window shows that reliable communication (low PER) is achieved for signal levels above approximately -115 dBm and for frequency offsets within roughly ±25 ppm. Outside this area, the PER increases sharply.
