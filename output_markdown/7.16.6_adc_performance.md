**Detailed Description of ADC Performance Graphs**

This image displays six graphs illustrating the performance characteristics of the ADC under various conditions.

### Figure 7-22. ENOB vs. Input Frequency
This graph shows the Effective Number of Bits (ENOB) as a function of the input signal frequency.
*   **X-axis:** Frequency in kHz, on a logarithmic scale from 0.2 kHz to 100 kHz.
*   **Y-axis:** ENOB in Bits, from 9.6 to 11.4.
*   **Conditions:** Two curves are plotted:
    *   **Internal Unscaled Reference, 14-bit Mode (pink line with asterisks):** ENOB is relatively stable, staying around 11.1 to 11.2 bits across the entire frequency range, with a slight drop-off starting around 70 kHz.
    *   **Internal Reference, No Averaging (red line):** ENOB starts around 9.9 bits at 0.2 kHz, dips slightly, and remains mostly flat around 9.9 bits until 40 kHz, after which it becomes more erratic and drops to about 9.7 bits at 100 kHz.

### Figure 7-23. ENOB vs. Sampling Frequency
This graph shows the ENOB as a function of the ADC's sampling frequency.
*   **Conditions:** Input is a 3.0 V sine wave, using the internal reference, with the input frequency (Fin) set to one-tenth of the sampling frequency (Fs).
*   **X-axis:** Sampling Frequency in kHz, on a logarithmic scale from 1 kHz to 200 kHz.
*   **Y-axis:** ENOB in Bits, from 9.8 to 10.2.
*   **Trend:** ENOB starts just above 10.0 bits at 1 kHz, dips to a low of approximately 9.85 bits at 4 kHz, and then gradually rises and stabilizes around 9.95 bits for sampling frequencies from 10 kHz to 200 kHz.

### Figure 7-24. INL vs. ADC Code
This graph plots the Integral Nonlinearity (INL) error across the range of ADC output codes.
*   **Conditions:** Input is a 3.0 V sine wave, using the internal reference, with a sampling rate of 200 kSamples/s.
*   **X-axis:** ADC Code, from 0 to 4000.
*   **Y-axis:** INL in LSB (Least Significant Bits), from -1.5 to 1.5.
*   **Trend:** The INL shows a distinct periodic, sawtooth-like pattern. The error cycles between approximately -1.2 LSB and +1.2 LSB. The pattern repeats roughly four times over the 0-4096 code range.

### Figure 7-25. DNL vs. ADC Code
This graph plots the Differential Nonlinearity (DNL) error across the range of ADC output codes.
*   **Conditions:** Input is a 3.0 V sine wave, using the internal reference, with a sampling rate of 200 kSamples/s.
*   **X-axis:** ADC Code, from 0 to 4000.
*   **Y-axis:** DNL in LSB, from -0.5 to 2.5.
*   **Trend:** The DNL is mostly centered around 0 LSB, indicating good code-to-code transition performance. There are several positive spikes that reach up to +2 LSB, but the vast majority of values are within +/- 0.5 LSB.

### Figure 7-26. ADC Accuracy vs. Temperature
This graph shows the measured voltage for a fixed input voltage across a range of operating temperatures.
*   **Conditions:** Input voltage (Vin) is 1 V, using the internal reference, with a sampling rate of 200 kSamples/s.
*   **X-axis:** Temperature in degrees Celsius, from -40°C to 100°C.
*   **Y-axis:** Measured Voltage in Volts, from 1.000 V to 1.010 V.
*   **Trend:** The measured voltage exhibits a slight parabolic curve. It is highest at the temperature extremes (approx. 1.008 V at -40°C and 100°C) and lowest in the middle of the range (approx. 1.006 V around 30°C to 40°C).

### Figure 7-27. ADC Accuracy vs. Supply Voltage (VDDS)
This graph shows the measured voltage for a fixed input voltage across a range of supply voltages.
*   **Conditions:** Input voltage (Vin) is 1 V, using the internal reference, with a sampling rate of 200 kSamples/s.
*   **X-axis:** Supply Voltage (VDDS) in Volts, from 1.8 V to 3.8 V.
*   **Y-axis:** Measured Voltage in Volts, from 1.000 V to 1.010 V.
*   **Trend:** The measured voltage is very stable across the supply voltage range. It varies only slightly, from a high of about 1.0075 V at 1.8V to a low of about 1.0065 V around 2.8V, before rising slightly again. This demonstrates good power supply rejection.
